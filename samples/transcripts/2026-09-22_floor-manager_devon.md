A Saturday is about a hundred and eighty covers. Weeknights, a hundred to a hundred and twenty.

**Maya** [00:05:45]: What's good about SeatNow?

**Devon** [00:05:58]: The table management is solid. I can see the whole floor, who's seated, who's on dessert, who's asked for the check. And the SMS reminders, those are the thing. Before SMS reminders our no-shows were around twelve percent. Now it's more like six, seven.

**Maya** [00:07:20]: So roughly cut in half.

**Devon** [00:07:28]: Yeah. And that is, on a Saturday, maybe ten or eleven empty seats less. At an average check of sixty-eight a head, that's, what, seven hundred bucks a weekend night. So the SMS fee is nothing. They charge us, I think, like four cents a text. Whatever. I'd pay double.

**Maya** [00:09:03]: That's a strong signal. Do you ever think about the cost?

**Devon** [00:09:21]: No. Not on SMS. If I could pay ten cents a text and get it to three percent no-shows, I'd do it tomorrow. It's the cheapest thing in the building. Compare it to a line cook's hour.

**Maya** [00:10:21]: Okay. What are the big pains today?

**Maya** [00:12:00]: Tell me about the schedule. How is that connected?

**Devon** [00:12:18]: So I build the weekly schedule on Sunday night. In a Google Sheet. I look at the booked covers for the next week in SeatNow, I look at last year's numbers, and I guess. Like, how many servers on Thursday. It takes me maybe an hour and a half every Sunday, and I get it wrong about once a week. Either too many servers standing around, or two in the weeds. Each server is, like, a hundred and forty bucks for a shift, so overstaffing by two is almost three hundred.

**Maya** [00:14:31]: What would help?

**Devon** [00:14:44]: If the reservation system could say, "Hey, Thursday you have eighty covers booked, typically you end up with a hundred and ten, here's how many servers that is." It's a guess either way but I'd love a recommendation. Even just a forecast of covers by hour.

**Maya** [00:17:11]: Interesting. So the tool would need a quick way to add walk-ins.

**Devon** [00:17:28]: Yeah, a big button. "Add walk-in, party of two." Two taps.

**Maya** [00:17:54]: Let's go to deposits. What's your experience?

**Devon** [00:18:16]: Okay, so we're strongly pro deposit. We did a pilot last spring: ten dollars a head for any booking on a Friday or Saturday between seven and nine, refundable up to twenty-four hours before. The no-show rate in those slots went from eight percent to under two. And honestly the complaints were way fewer than I expected. Maybe one a week.

**Maya** [00:20:04]: What did people say in the complaints?

**Devon** [00:20:17]: Mostly "I don't want to put my card in." Some said "we're a restaurant, not a concert." One guy left a one-star review about it. But you take that trade. We also got a lot of "oh that's fair" because the price was low and refundable.

**Maya** [00:21:35]: So the refundable part matters.

**Devon** [00:21:47]: A lot. If it's non-refundable, I think you lose a chunk of people. Refundable until a day before, it just says "we're serious." The money's also credited toward the bill if they show.

**Maya** [00:22:52]: Do you do it for all bookings now?

**Devon** [00:23:05]: Only prime slots. And for parties of six-plus, any day. Not for early seating or Tuesday. You don't want to scare people when you need them.

**Maya** [00:23:57]: Do you think a smaller place, like a café, should do it?

**Devon** [00:24:19]: Eh. Depends on whether they have a no-show problem. If it's three percent, don't bother. I wouldn't. At ours it was a real number, we're a seafood place, we buy the fish for the night. If a table of eight doesn't show, that's wasted halibut.

**Maya** [00:25:32]: Ha. Alright. Switching to how guests book. What do you see?

**Devon** [00:25:54]: Ugh, I'd say fifty percent comes from the website and Google, thirty percent phone, and the rest are Instagram and email.
