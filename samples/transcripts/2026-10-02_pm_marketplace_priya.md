**Nina** [00:00:05]: Hi Priya! Can you hear me okay?

**Priya** [00:00:08]: Yes, hi. Sorry, one sec, I'm closing about forty tabs. Okay, I'm here.

**Nina** [00:00:16]: No rush. Is it fine if I record this?

**Priya** [00:00:19]: Of course. I record everything, it would be hypocritical to say no.

**Nina** [00:00:25]: Ha, good. So, I'm researching an early idea called Throughline: a workspace where BAs and PMs build personas, journey maps, hypotheses and roadmaps from research, with every item linked back to interview quotes. I'm not pitching, I want to learn how you do this. So, tell me about you.

**Priya** [00:00:50]: I'm a product manager at Cartwheel. It's a two-sided marketplace for secondhand furniture and home goods, sellers list, buyers browse, we run delivery logistics in six cities. About a hundred and eighty people. I own the seller side: listing flow, pricing tools, payouts. I've got a squad of seven engineers, one designer, and a researcher who's embedded half the time.

**Nina** [00:01:25]: You said you record everything. Describe your research cadence.

**Priya** [00:01:29]: So I follow the continuous discovery approach, loosely. Every week, I talk to at least two sellers, and my designer talks to at least two buyers or sellers, plus our researcher does her own thing. In a quarter, between us, we do about sixty to seventy interviews. Mine personally is about twenty-five to thirty. Mostly thirty minutes, recruited through an in-app prompt. And we have a standing rule: no sprint planning without at least one customer clip shown.

**Nina** [00:02:10]: Sixty to seventy, that's a lot. How do you manage the volume?

**Priya** [00:02:14]: Badly, sometimes. Zoom records, the transcript goes into Dovetail, I tag in Dovetail, highlight reels, and then I put the top insights into a Notion database called "Opportunities". It's a opportunity solution tree, kind of, in Miro as well, which I redraw every month. Then Linear for delivery. So that's Zoom, Dovetail, Notion, Miro, Linear. Five tools. I'd say I spend about nine hours a week in Dovetail and Notion combined, plus two in Miro.

**Nina** [00:02:55]: Where does it break down?

**Priya** [00:02:58]: The gap between Dovetail and everything else. I have an insight in Dovetail, "sellers abandon listings when asked for dimensions," I find it on the Notion board, I link it manually to a Linear ticket, and three weeks later someone asks why. And the link is to the whole Dovetail project, not to the clip. It doesn't survive.

**Nina** [00:03:25]: Let's talk personas. Do you do them?

**Priya** [00:03:29]: Yes, but I define them differently than most people. Not demographics. Behavioral segments, sort of. We have three seller types: the clear-out seller, someone moving house, they list thirty things in a weekend. The side-hustler, who flips furniture, sells two hundred things a year. And the occasional one-off seller. I built those by hand from about forty interviews, and I do mean by hand. Printed transcripts, a table, highlighters, the whole nonsense. It took me four weeks of evenings, partially.

**Nina** [00:04:15]: Why by hand?

**Priya** [00:04:18]: Because that's where the understanding happens. If I hadn't read each of those forty transcripts myself, and fought with my designer about whether the flipper and the clear-out seller were really different, I wouldn't believe the persona. The persona isn't the output. The argument is the output. The document is just the receipt.

**Nina** [00:04:45]: How often do you update them?

**Priya** [00:04:49]: Every quarter, a light refresh. Every six months, bigger. Every time we run a new cohort, I add the new interviews and see whether the segments still hold. That was a surprise last spring: a fourth segment emerged, people who sell on behalf of a relative, an estate clearing. We hadn't seen it, it showed up in six interviews in a row.

**Nina** [00:05:20]: And journey maps?

**Priya** [00:05:23]: One per core flow, listing a piece of furniture from photo to sold to paid out. Miro, built with the designer and the ops lead. It has the emotional curve and also the actual backend events. It's updated, I would say, every other month. Because the flow changes with every release.

**Nina** [00:05:52]: Hypotheses?

**Priya** [00:05:55]: In the opportunity tree. Each leaf is a hypothesis in the "we believe, because we saw" format. Some live in Notion, some in the Miro. I rate them on confidence. High, medium, low, and the confidence is an honest assessment of the evidence. How many interviews, how strong is the quote. If it's one quote, it's low. If it's six sellers independently, high.

**Nina** [00:06:35]: So evidence strength matters to you. Do you require a link to the source for every claim?

**Priya** [00:06:40]: For the load-bearing ones, yes. For the top ten opportunities, I want clips. For everything, no, that's absurd, I'd never finish. I have a BA friend at a bank who has a matrix with every claim sourced. I couldn't live like that. But a clip, thirty seconds of a seller saying it in their own voice, is the most persuasive object in product management.

**Nina** [00:07:15]: Interesting. Now, executives. How do you share all this upward?

**Priya** [00:07:20]: Monthly, I do a seller-side review with the VP of product and the COO. I bring the roadmap as three horizons, now, next, later. And, here's the thing, the execs don't want the roadmap, they want the story. I open with two minutes of clips. A seller crying about a failed payout. Then the metric, then the bet. If I open with a slide of themes they check their phones. If I open with a voice, they listen.

**Nina** [00:07:58]: What would help you in that meeting?

**Priya** [00:08:01]: Being able to pull the three best clips for a roadmap item in one click, from a single place. Right now I scrub through recordings. Twenty minutes per item, and I'd do six items. So two hours before a monthly meeting, just scrubbing.

**Nina** [00:08:25]: Who owns the roadmap, in your view?

**Priya** [00:08:28]: I do, on my side. We do have BAs on the finance and logistics teams because those systems are complicated, and they own requirements and edge cases. I don't want them deciding what to build. But I want them in the discovery. Honestly, the best BAs I've worked with sit in my interviews.

**Nina** [00:08:55]: Now the idea I'm testing. A tool that takes the transcripts and auto-generates themes, quotes, even draft personas and journey maps. How does that land?

**Priya** [00:09:08]: Mixed, and I want to be careful. For tagging and clip-finding, yes, please, I'd pay for that. Transcribing and surfacing "here are eleven moments where sellers mention photos" saves me hours. But the persona, the synthesis, the actual meaning? No. A persona I didn't build, I wouldn't trust. I'd also have a problem with my team: if I say "the persona says" and someone asks how I know and I answer "the tool generated it," I've lost the room. The sensemaking is the work. If you automate it, you automate away the part that makes me good at the job.

**Nina** [00:10:00]: So the line is tagging versus synthesis.

**Priya** [00:10:04]: Roughly. Though a draft that I could shred and rewrite? Maybe. If it's clearly labeled as a draft and I can see what it was built from. But I worry that people won't shred it. They'll just paste it into a deck. That's what I'd be afraid of for junior PMs on my team: they take the AI persona as the answer.

**Nina** [00:10:40]: How do you feel about a dedicated tool versus your stack?

**Priya** [00:10:44]: I'm open. Five tools is silly. I would happily replace Dovetail and the Notion database with one thing, if it could do clips, tags, opportunity tree and push to Linear. What I don't want is a sixth tool. It has to replace something. And Miro for the live workshops, I wouldn't give up. Whiteboarding is human.

**Nina** [00:11:20]: What would it be worth to you?

**Priya** [00:11:24]: Dovetail is, I think, about thirty a seat for us, we have six seats. If one tool replaced Dovetail and my Notion mess, I'd pay forty, forty-five per seat per month, and I wouldn't blink. Fifty-five, I'd have to justify it. For the team, say ten people, so four to five hundred a month. I'd go higher if it integrated with Linear, genuinely, because that integration is where my three weeks of "why did we do this" lives.

**Nina** [00:12:05]: Good. What do you like in the current workflow?

**Priya** [00:12:09]: Dovetail's highlight reels. Honestly, that's the best part of my process. And the discipline of the weekly interviews. The tool doesn't matter, the habit does. If I hadn't had the habit I wouldn't have found the estate-clearing segment.

**Nina** [00:12:35]: What do you hate most?

**Priya** [00:12:39]: Recruiting, actually. Not a tool thing, a logistics thing. And, yes, the redrawing of the tree in Miro each month. And duplicate findings. Three people tag the same insight three different ways and the tree gets messy.

**Nina** [00:13:05]: Is there a job you're trying to get done that tools don't help with?

**Priya** [00:13:10]: "Get everyone to see what I saw." That's it. Research is only valuable if the team shares the understanding. The tool should make it easy for an engineer to drop in, watch a two-minute clip, and feel it. I've seen engineers change an implementation because they heard a seller sigh.

**Nina** [00:13:40]: Beautiful. Last question, anything I should have asked?

**Priya** [00:13:44]: Maybe whether I'd let a vendor hold my interview recordings. Consent. Our sellers agree to research, but I'd want clear retention rules and delete-on-request. Not a blocker, just a checkbox I'd check first.

**Nina** [00:14:05]: Thank you, that's helpful. Thanks so much, Priya.

**Priya** [00:14:09]: Anytime. Send me the prototype, I'll break it.
