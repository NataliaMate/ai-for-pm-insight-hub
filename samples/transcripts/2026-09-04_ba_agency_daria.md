**Nina** [00:00:05]: Okay, I think the recording's going. Can you hear me alright?

**Daria** [00:00:08]: Yeah, loud and clear. Sorry, I'm in the little phone-booth room at the office, so if someone knocks, that's why.

**Nina** [00:00:15]: No worries at all. Thanks for making time. Quick context: I work on a product called Throughline. It's very early, we're mostly trying to find out if it's even a real problem. It's a workspace for BAs and PMs to build personas, journey maps, hypotheses and roadmaps from research. I'm not selling anything today, I just want to hear how you do it right now. There are no wrong answers, and you can't hurt my feelings.

**Daria** [00:00:42]: Good, because I have opinions.

**Nina** [00:00:45]: Perfect. So, tell me about your role and what a normal week looks like.

**Daria** [00:00:52]: Um, so I'm a business analyst at Northbridge Digital, it's a digital agency, about sixty people. We build websites, portals, a few apps, for clients. Right now I'm on four projects at the same time, which is, honestly, one too many. A retail client, a regional energy company, a nonprofit, and a small fintech thing. I'm the only BA on three of them. So a normal week is a lot of context switching. Monday is usually the energy client because their stakeholders only do Mondays, which is its own saga.

**Nina** [00:01:30]: Four at once. How much of that is research, versus delivery?

**Daria** [00:01:36]: Ha. Less than I'd like. If I'm honest, maybe six, seven hours a week is actual research, talking to users. The rest is, uh, making artifacts. Maybe twelve hours a week in Miro and Slides and Notion just arranging things so a client can look at them.

**Nina** [00:01:58]: Twelve hours arranging. Let's go there. When a new project starts, how do you build a persona?

**Daria** [00:02:06]: Okay. So at kickoff I get, like, whatever the client has. Usually a deck from marketing with "segments" that are basically demographics. Then I run maybe five to eight interviews, sometimes the client gives me contacts, sometimes I recruit through UserTesting if the budget allows, which is rare. I record on Zoom, I take notes in Notion, one page per interview. Then I do the sticky-note thing in Miro. I copy the notes out of Notion by hand, paste them as stickies, cluster them. That clustering takes me, I don't know, a full day for six interviews.

**Nina** [00:02:50]: And the persona itself?

**Daria** [00:02:53]: Then I go to Google Slides because clients want a pretty one-pager. Name, photo from a stock site, goals, frustrations, a quote. And, yeah, the quote is where I always waste time. I'm scrolling back through the Notion pages going, where did they say that thing about the invoice? Because I know somebody said it, I just can't find which interview.

**Nina** [00:03:25]: So the quote is detached from the source.

**Daria** [00:03:28]: Completely. Once it's on the slide it's just text. If the client asks "who said this", I have to go hunting. Twice it's been embarrassing. Once I attributed a quote to the wrong person's segment and the client caught it in the room.

**Nina** [00:03:48]: Ouch. What about journey maps?

**Daria** [00:03:52]: Journey maps are Miro again, I have a template I've been reusing for two years. Swim lanes: stages across the top, then actions, thoughts, pain points, opportunities. I redo it per project, I never keep one for long. The painful bit is moving anything from the journey map into Jira. A pain point in Miro becomes an epic in Jira, and I retype it. And then in Jira nobody knows where it came from.

**Nina** [00:04:30]: How often do those maps get updated after the workshop?

**Daria** [00:04:34]: Honestly? Once. Like, the version we present. Then we go into build and the map just sits in the Miro board. I would say, at the agency, a persona is updated maybe never. Maybe if the client is a long retainer, once or twice a year. Because we're not paid to maintain them, that's the thing. They're a deliverable, not a living thing.

**Nina** [00:05:05]: Interesting. Let's talk hypotheses. Do you write them down anywhere?

**Daria** [00:05:10]: Hmm. Sort of. Not formally. I have a Notion table called "Assumptions", with columns like assumption, how risky, how we'd test it. For two of the four projects it exists. For the others it's in my head, which I know is bad. I have tried the "we believe that, if we, then, we'll know when" format, and clients glaze over. So I keep it in plain words.

**Nina** [00:05:48]: And do the hypotheses connect to the interviews?

**Daria** [00:05:52]: No. I mean, I put a link in the cell sometimes. A Notion link to the interview page. But not to the exact moment. And, sorry, a tangent, but I once had a client who wanted me to prove every statement, and I spent a weekend building a spreadsheet of "evidence", and then they never opened it. So now I only do it when somebody pushes.

**Nina** [00:06:25]: Noted. And roadmaps and backlog?

**Daria** [00:06:29]: So roadmap is the client's, or the agency PM's, I don't really own it. I propose, they decide. The backlog I do own, mostly. It's in Jira. For the smaller clients I use a Google Sheet because they don't want a Jira license. I write epics and user stories, I attach the acceptance criteria. And then I'm in a prioritization workshop where we use, like, a two-by-two in Miro. Value versus effort. Not RICE, honestly. I know RICE exists, I've done it twice, the scores always end up being argued rather than calculated.

**Nina** [00:07:15]: Say more about that, the argued scores.

**Daria** [00:07:18]: Reach is fine, you can guess it. Impact is, like, vibes. And then the client's director says "that's a ten for us" and it's a ten. So the tool does nothing, it's just theater with numbers.

**Nina** [00:07:40]: If you could wave a wand, what would you want from that whole chain?

**Daria** [00:07:45]: Okay, honestly, one place. Where I drop the interview, it gets transcribed, I highlight a line, and that line can become a quote on the persona, a pain point on the journey, and the source for the epic. And it all keeps its link. That's it. If I had that I'd save, I mean, probably a day per project at the synthesis stage. A day and a half.

**Nina** [00:08:20]: What about the synthesis itself, the clustering? Would you want a tool to do that for you?

**Daria** [00:08:25]: Yes. Absolutely. Like, auto-cluster my notes, propose a first-draft persona. I would still check it, obviously, I'm not going to send a hallucination to a client. But I'd rather edit a draft than stare at a blank Miro board at nine at night. I tried pasting interview notes into a chatbot and it was like eighty percent right. The twenty percent was the stuff that mattered, but still, it got me started. So the thing I'd need is I can see where each sentence came from.

**Nina** [00:09:05]: So draft plus citations, you're comfortable.

**Daria** [00:09:08]: Yes. A draft I can see the receipts for, yes. A black box, no.

**Nina** [00:09:15]: What do you currently pay for in this space?

**Daria** [00:09:19]: Miro, the agency pays, I think it's around fifteen, sixteen dollars a seat per month, and we have maybe twenty seats. Notion is, uh, eight or ten a seat. Jira I don't know, finance handles it. I did trial Dovetail once. I loved the tagging, but it didn't connect to anything, it was another island. I'd be copying out of it again.

**Nina** [00:09:55]: Right. So an island problem. How much would you pay for the thing you described?

**Daria** [00:10:00]: Hmm. For me, if I could expense it, I'd say twenty-five a month and I wouldn't ask anyone. Thirty-five, I'd have to ask my director, and I'd need to show I saved the day per project. Above fifty, no. At the agency we bill by the hour so I could actually put a number on it: a day saved is, whatever, six hundred euros of billable time. So maybe it pays for itself. But the agency is stingy about seat licenses.

**Nina** [00:10:42]: That's useful. Is there anything about the stack you'd say actually works well?

**Daria** [00:10:47]: Miro for workshops, genuinely. Clients love sticking things on a board together, it's participatory. And Notion for notes is fast. I wouldn't want to lose the collaborative whiteboard part. If your tool is just forms and tables, I'd miss that.

**Nina** [00:11:15]: Good to know. What's the worst moment in the process? The one that makes you want to quit?

**Daria** [00:11:20]: Handoff. Definitely. When the project goes to the dev team and the developers ask "why are we building this", and I have to reconstruct the reasoning from, like, three tools. Last month I spent forty-five minutes on a call with a dev lead explaining where one requirement came from, and I found the quote in a Zoom recording at minute fifty-something. It's just, it's all so manual.

**Nina** [00:11:58]: Is that a job you hire someone to do? Like "prove the requirement is grounded"?

**Daria** [00:12:03]: Yeah, I'd say my job is, make the client confident that we're building the right thing. And, secondly, protect myself. Be able to say, here, this is why.

**Nina** [00:12:20]: Okay. A few quick ones. Team size?

**Daria** [00:12:23]: Agency of sixty, there are four BAs, two PMs, designers... Per project it's me plus a designer plus a PM, three to five devs.

**Nina** [00:12:38]: And how many interviews do you run a quarter, across all projects?

**Daria** [00:12:42]: Across the four? Maybe twenty-five. Some quarters fifteen. It really depends on whether a client lets me talk to users, because many of them don't. They say "we know our customers". Then I'm interviewing the client's support team instead.

**Nina** [00:13:05]: That's a funny aside, is that common?

**Daria** [00:13:08]: Super common. I have a theory that half of agency "user research" is actually stakeholder research. Anyway.

**Nina** [00:13:20]: Final area. Would you worry about a persona your tool generated being generic? Like, the stock persona?

**Daria** [00:13:28]: I'd worry if it had no grounding. If I can see "this persona is built from interviews two, four and five" and click through, I'm fine. Otherwise it's the same stock Sarah-from-marketing that every deck has.

**Nina** [00:13:48]: Last question. Anything I haven't asked that I should have?

**Daria** [00:13:52]: Mobile, maybe. Clients review on their phones, in a taxi, and Miro is awful on a phone. If the persona or journey view was readable on a phone, that alone would be a win. And, sorry, one more, sharing with a client without giving them an account. A public link. Because the client's legal team will not let their people create accounts, and then I end up exporting a PDF, and then the PDF is stale.

**Nina** [00:14:30]: Both great. Thank you, Daria, this was really useful.

**Daria** [00:14:34]: Of course. Send me the beta when you have it. I'll be your difficult tester.

**Nina** [00:14:40]: Deal.
