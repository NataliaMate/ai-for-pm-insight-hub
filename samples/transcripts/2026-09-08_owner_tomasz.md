**Tomasz** [00:05:12]: The shop phone. It's the business app, the green one, I put the opening hours in. Nothing fancy. People write "table for four Friday?" and I write back yes or no and then I copy it into the book. By hand.

**Maya** [00:06:12]: How long does that take you a day?

**Tomasz** [00:06:23]: Hmm. Honestly? Maybe ten, fifteen minutes in the morning and some in the evening. It's not a big job. Thursday to Saturday there's maybe eighteen, twenty bookings for the supper plus some tables. So it's, yeah. I don't think it's a problem. My wife thinks it is.

**Maya** [00:07:34]: Why does she think it's a problem?

**Tomasz** [00:07:44]: Because I once wrote Friday in the Thursday page. Fourteen people turned up and the book said six. That was a bad night. We borrowed chairs from the florist next door. So, okay, it's a problem sometimes. Twice a year, maybe. Three times.

**Maya** [00:08:52]: What would you say your no-show rate is?

**Tomasz** [00:09:06]: For the supper, I would say, two, three people a month don't come. Out of, what, ninety? So three percent? Four? The food is already cooked for them, it hurts, but I'm not going to build a whole system around three percent.

**Maya** [00:10:10]: Do you do anything to reduce it?

**Tomasz** [00:10:20]: I write them on the day. "See you tonight at seven, yes?" In WhatsApp. It takes me five minutes and most reply with a thumbs-up. The ones who don't reply, I call.

**Maya** [00:11:06]: So you already do reminders manually.

**Tomasz** [00:11:17]: Yes. And they work. People like a human message. I think a robot text would feel cold for a place like ours.

**Maya** [00:11:49]: Interesting. What about software, have you ever tried any?

**Tomasz** [00:12:03]: I tried one, SeatNow, for a month. The free trial. It was fine. But then they wanted forty-nine dollars a month and I thought, forty-nine dollars, that's twelve loaves of my sourdough sold at full price, every week, just to have a calendar. No. I stopped the trial the day before.

**Maya** [00:13:25]: Forty-nine a month. Was that the price you'd be willing to pay, or too high?

**Tomasz** [00:13:42]: Too high. For what I do, like, fifteen dollars, I would think about it. Twenty, at most. If it saved me the wrong-page problem. Above that, no, because my profit on the supper is not that large. It's a nice little thing, it's not the whole business.

**Maya** [00:14:53]: That's really helpful. What did you dislike about SeatNow, apart from price?

**Tomasz** [00:15:11]: Too many buttons. It wanted me to draw my floor plan, set up the "turn times," the "zones." I have a long table. One table. And the reminders were by SMS and they said it costs extra. I said no thank you.

**Maya** [00:16:15]: Would you pay for SMS reminders?

**Tomasz** [00:16:26]: No. Why? WhatsApp is free. Everybody has it. I write to them there, they write back there. Why would I pay for a text message? It's, uh, it's like paying for a stamp when I can walk over.

**Maya** [00:17:22]: Okay, that's clear. And deposits, have you ever asked people for money up front?

**Tomasz** [00:17:44]: No. No no. Absolutely not. If I say "send me twenty dollars before you come," half of them go to the other café. We're small. People come here because it's friendly. Asking for a deposit, it's like asking them to prove they're not criminals. I'd rather lose three percent than lose the mood.

**Maya** [00:19:02]: Got it. But the long supper table, the food is prepared for them. Does that change anything?

**Maya** [00:22:10]: Ha. Okay, so what would make a reservation tool worth it for you? Imagine something existed. What would it have to do?

**Tomasz** [00:22:38]: Honestly? Be a WhatsApp thing. If a person writes to me, I press one button, it goes in a calendar, and it shows me the days. That's it. And it must not make me draw anything. And no screen with twenty options. If I need a tutorial, I'm out.

**Maya** [00:23:49]: So capturing the WhatsApp message directly into the book.
